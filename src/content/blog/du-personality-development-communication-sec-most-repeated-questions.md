Personality Development and Communication (UPC **2036000005**) is a Skill Enhancement Course that students from many programmes take. It is a writing-and-speaking paper: part theory (barriers, body language, negotiation, selling) and part practical writing (a letter, a report, a questionnaire). That makes it very predictable.

We collected every paper we have for this code, from the 2022-23 sittings to May-June 2026, and removed duplicate scans. That left **25 distinct question papers**: 18 for regular students (1 hour, 30 marks) and 7 for School of Open Learning (SOL) students (2 hours, 80 marks).

All of them are free on the [Personality Development and Communication SEC PYQ page](https://www.dupyq.online/papers/skill-enhancement-courses/personality-development-and-communication). Open a few alongside this post.

Two notes on the counts. "14 of 25" means 14 of the 25 distinct papers mention that topic somewhere (a short note or a full question), not 14 exam sittings. And years are the labels on our paper pages: some college-library scans are labelled with the year they were scanned, so a paper marked 2024 may be from the sitting before.

## What the paper looks like

| Version | Time | Marks | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Regular (Common Programme Group)** | 1 hour | 30 | Five or six questions; **answer any three** at 10 marks each. Usually 3 to 4 theory questions, one **report or letter**, and often a **short-notes** question (any two, 5 + 5) |
| **B.A. (Hons.) version** | 1 hour | 30 | Answer all: Q1 and Q2 each give an **"OR" choice** (10 marks each), then **any two short notes** (5 + 5) |
| **SOL** | 2 hours | 80 | Six questions, **answer any four** at 20 marks each. Always includes a **letter**, usually a **report or questionnaire**, and sometimes a **short note on any four of five** |

DU's syllabus for this course has four units: communication basics and types; business letters, presentations and reports; barriers, body language, teams and conflict; and negotiation and selling. Nearly every question traces back to one of them.

## Questions that were repeated across papers

These are the same question (sometimes word for word), found in papers from different years.

- **The Nov-Dec 2025 SOL paper reuses the Nov-Dec 2024 SOL paper.** Five of six questions are the same: "What role does body language play to build rapport in a business setting?", "Write a letter to an editor on the growing number of accidents in your city", "You are a marketing manager ... discuss five innovative digital marketing strategies", the **short note on any four** (audio-visual aids, intercultural communication, negotiation as a strategy, barriers of communication, memos and notices), and "Discuss three types of common office work related conflicts and how to cope with them". Only the second question differs.
- **A 2025 regular paper repeats pieces of the same set:** the non-verbal-cues-and-rapport question, the letter to an editor on accidents, and the short note "audio-visual aids, intercultural communication, negotiation as a strategy, barriers of communication" appear again.
- **A May-June 2024 regular paper and a 2025 regular paper share their middle section:** "Explain the following with suitable examples: (a) Body Language and Non-verbal Communication (b) Features of an Effective Presentation" and the short notes **Types of Conflict, Visual Communication, Memos and Circulars, Negotiation Skills** are word for word in both.
- **"Not everything is negotiable, do you agree or disagree? Explain two examples to illustrate your point"** is in Nov-Dec 2024 (regular) and Nov-Dec 2025 (SOL).
- **"Explain how social conventions, values, and perceptions affect communication"** is in 2022-23, May-June 2025 and May-June 2026.
- **"What is the role of body language in effective communication? Discuss with at least five examples"** is word for word in May-June 2025 and in a 2025 paper.
- **"What is the difference between a circular and a memo? Explain with suitable examples"** is in May-June 2025 and May-June 2026.
- **"Discuss how constructive feedback can enhance communication effectiveness and provide examples of its application in various contexts"** is in Nov-Dec 2024 and May-June 2026.
- **"Prepare a questionnaire to survey the demand for a portable and wearable air purifier in Delhi"** is in two May-June 2026 papers. A questionnaire question appears in 2024, 2025 and 2026 with a new topic each time.
- **"Write a formal letter to the Principal of your college seeking permission to hold a Students' Seminar"** is in May-June 2026 and in a 2024 paper.
- **"You developed an app. Explain the promotional strategies / strategies to sell it to your friends and peers"** is in a 2024 paper, a 2025 paper and May-June 2026.
- **"Discuss paralanguage"** is in May-June 2026 (twice) and in 2022-23.

The pattern: the paper is rebuilt from a small pool each sitting, and whole sections come back unchanged.

## Short notes that keep coming back

For the "short note on any two / any four" question, the same notes recur:

- **Barriers of communication**
- **Negotiation as a strategy / Negotiation skills**
- **Audio-visual aids in presentation / Features of an effective presentation**
- **Memos and circulars (or notices)**
- **Types of conflict**
- **Intercultural communication**
- **Body language and non-verbal communication**
- **Visual communication**
- **Selling skills / Conceptual selling / Strategic selling**
- **Emotional intelligence, Active listening, Importance of feedback, Leadership skills**

## The three writing tasks: how to do them

Most papers include at least one of these, and they are the easiest marks if you practise.

- **Letter:** sender's address, date, receiver's address, subject line, salutation, body in 3 short paragraphs (the problem, the details, what you want), "Yours faithfully" and name. Typical topics: to the Principal, an editor, a municipal body, a bank.
- **Report:** a heading, who wrote it and for whom, date, then **what happened, who took part, outcome and recommendation.** Typical topics: an NSS event, a blood-donation drive, a college trip.
- **Questionnaire:** a short introduction, then **at least ten questions** in a clear order (simple first), with answer options for the multiple-choice ones, and a thank-you. The paper often asks for "ten questions and answers".

## How to use this for your exam

1. **Prepare one model letter, one report and one questionnaire.** They are the least predictable-in-topic but most predictable-in-format marks.
2. **Write answers for the repeated questions above.** They cover most of the theory.
3. **Learn five short notes in 5 or 6 lines each:** barriers, negotiation, presentation, memo and circular, types of conflict.
4. **Add an example to every answer.** Many questions say "with suitable examples", and answers without them lose marks.
5. **SOL students:** you answer four of six at 20 marks, so each answer needs to be about twice as long and cover more points.

Past repetition does not guarantee what comes next. Use this list to decide what to revise *first*.
