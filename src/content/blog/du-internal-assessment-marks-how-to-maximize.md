Internal Assessment (IA) marks are the most overlooked scoring opportunity in Delhi University. While students spend weeks preparing for the 75-mark theory exam, the 25-mark internal assessment is treated as an afterthought. This is a strategic mistake — IA marks are the easiest marks you will ever earn at DU, and losing them forces you to score unrealistically high in the theory paper to compensate.

This guide breaks down exactly how DU's internal assessment system works and how to guarantee maximum marks every semester.

---

## How Internal Assessment is Structured

Under the UGCF system, internal assessment typically accounts for **25 marks out of 100** for each paper. The remaining 75 marks come from the end-of-semester theory examination.

The 25 marks are distributed as follows (may vary slightly by college):

| Component | Marks | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Attendance | 5 marks | Based on percentage of classes attended |
| Class Tests / Mid-Semester Test | 10 marks | Best of 2 class tests, or a single mid-term |
| Assignment / Presentation / Project | 10 marks | Written assignment or group presentation |

---

## Component 1: Attendance (5 Marks)

The attendance grading scale used by most DU colleges:

| Attendance % | Marks Awarded |
|:---:|:---:|
| 85% and above | 5/5 |
| 75% to 84% | 4/5 |
| 65% to 74% | 3/5 |
| 55% to 64% | 2/5 |
| Below 55% | 1/5 or 0 |

### How to Maximize
- **Target 85%+ attendance** in every paper. This guarantees full 5 marks with zero academic effort.
- Track your attendance weekly. Most DU colleges now upload attendance on the department portal — check it regularly and raise discrepancies immediately.
- If you must miss classes (for medical reasons, society events, or placement interviews), submit the required leave applications promptly.

---

## Component 2: Class Tests (10 Marks)

Most colleges conduct 2 class tests per semester and consider the **best score**. Some colleges conduct a single mid-semester test worth 10 marks.

### How to Maximize
- **Treat class tests as mini exams.** Revise the relevant units 2–3 days before the test. Most class tests cover only 1–2 units, making them much easier to prepare for than the full semester exam.
- **Write in exam format.** Use subheadings, underline key terms, and add diagrams. Even in a 30-minute class test, structured answers score higher.
- **If your college takes the best of 2 tests,** the second test is your safety net. If you scored poorly in Test 1, a strong Test 2 replaces it entirely.

---

## Component 3: Assignments (10 Marks)

Assignments can take several forms: written essays, presentations, research papers, or practical projects.

### How to Maximize
- **Submit on time.** Late submissions often receive automatic mark deductions (typically 1–2 marks per day late).
- **Format properly.** Use a cover page, table of contents, and numbered references. Visual presentation matters — a neat, typed assignment scores better than a handwritten one with crossed-out sections.
- **Cite sources.** Even a simple footnote like *"Source: NCERT Economics Textbook, Chapter 3"* elevates the quality of your assignment in the professor's eyes.
- **For presentations:** Practice your delivery once before presenting. Maintain eye contact, speak clearly, and keep slides clean with minimal text.

---

## The Math: Why IA Marks Are Critical

Consider two students:
- **Student A** scores 23/25 in IA and 50/75 in the theory exam = **73/100 (A grade, 8 points)**
- **Student B** scores 15/25 in IA and 50/75 in the theory exam = **65/100 (B+ grade, 7 points)**

Both scored identically in the theory exam, but Student A's higher IA gave them a **full grade point advantage**. Over 5–6 papers in a semester, this difference translates to 0.5–1.0 SGPA points.

---

## When Are IA Marks Finalized?

Internal assessment marks are submitted to the DU examination portal by each college **before the end-of-semester theory exams begin**. Once submitted, they are generally final — you cannot retroactively improve your IA marks.

If you believe your IA marks have been calculated incorrectly, raise the issue with your department coordinator *before* the submission deadline.
