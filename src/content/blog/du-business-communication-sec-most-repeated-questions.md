Business Communication (Unique Paper Code **2416000001**) is a Skill Enhancement Course (SEC) taken by B.A. (Prog.) and other common-programme students. It is a scoring paper if you know what the examiners want, and in 2026 what they want changed.

We compared every distinct paper we have for this code: **26 papers, from Nov-Dec 2023 to May-Jun 2026**, with duplicate copies removed. All of them are on the [Business Communication PYQ page](https://www.dupyq.online/papers/skill-enhancement-courses/business-communication).

## The big change: from "explain and draft" to "analyse the situation"

Until May-Jun 2025 the paper asked you to *explain* a concept, *draft* a letter, notice or minutes, or *write short notes*, in 6- and 12-mark questions with an OR choice.

| Period | What the questions looked like | Papers using "analyse / examine / critically / evaluate" |
|---|---|---|
| Nov-Dec 2023 to May-Jun 2025 (18 papers, 6 of them without a clear sitting label) | "Distinguish oral and written communication" · "Draft minutes of a Board meeting" · "Write a notice on Swachh Bharat" · "Explain the 7 Cs" | **0 of 18** |
| Nov-Dec 2025 | Scenario and statement questions begin: "A newly appointed manager faces barriers... analyse and suggest measures" · "Draft a resume" · "suggest modern AI tools for a researcher" | 1 of 2 |
| May-Jun 2026 | "A researcher uses multiple sources but fails to cite them. Analyse the consequences" · "A team meeting becomes ineffective... analyse causes and suggest solutions" | **6 of 6** |

The topics did not change much. **The way they are asked did.** A scenario question gives you a situation and wants you to apply the concept to it.

The Nov-Dec 2025 common-programme papers used a different split: attempt any four of six questions (60 marks, 15 each), or any three of five (30 marks, 10 each). Check the instructions on your paper.

Three things stand out:

- **Referencing is the one topic that was in every 2026 paper**, as a full question, a Q1 item, or both. Bibliography, citation and referencing came in 5 of the 6 Q1 lists.
- **Channels of communication and interviews were less common earlier and are now in most papers.** Expect the "poor information flow in a company" and "non-verbal communication in interviews" scenarios again.
- **Drafting topics (minutes, notices, emails) are in none of the six 2026 papers**, but they were in most earlier ones. The Nov-Dec 2025 papers still asked for a resume and annual-report principles, so keep one drafting format ready.

## Scenario questions from the 2026 papers

These show the pattern. The same concepts will come back with new situations.

- *A company faces internal conflict because of poor information flow.* Examine how the different **directions of communication** could fix it.
- *A researcher uses many sources but cites them badly.* Analyse why **referencing standards** matter.
- *"Non-verbal communication often speaks louder than words."* Discuss for **interviews and meetings**.
- *"Negotiation is more about strategy than communication."* Critically evaluate.
- *A project fails because employees misunderstood written instructions.* Analyse the importance of **clarity**.
- *A team meeting is ineffective because of poor interpersonal communication.* Analyse causes and suggest solutions.
- *"Technology has made communication faster but not necessarily better."* Discuss with reference to **business correspondence**.

## How to answer a scenario question

Use four steps, about a paragraph each for a 10-mark answer:

1. **Concept.** Define the idea the situation is testing (for example, downward and upward communication).
2. **Diagnose.** Say what went wrong in this case, using the facts given.
3. **Apply.** Show how the concept explains it, or how it would fix it.
4. **Recommend and conclude.** Two or three specific actions, then one closing line.

## Quick pointers for the repeating topics

These are revision prompts. Write the full answer from your course reader.

**Communication process.** Sender, encoding, message, channel, receiver, decoding, feedback, and noise at every stage.

**Directions of communication.** Downward (instructions, policies), upward (reports, suggestions), horizontal or lateral (between peers), diagonal. Formal channels follow the hierarchy; informal ones (the grapevine) do not. Poor flow causes delays, rumours and conflict, and the fix is two-way feedback.

**Non-verbal communication.** Body language (kinesics), tone and pitch (paralanguage), space (proxemics), time (chronemics), appearance. In interviews it shows confidence and honesty, and it should match your words.

**Referencing.** A bibliography lists everything you read; references list what you cited in the text; footnotes and endnotes give notes on a point. Name one style (APA is common) and explain why citing avoids plagiarism, lets readers verify, and credits the author.

**Negotiation.** Preparation, discussion, bargaining, agreement. Distributive (win-lose) vs integrative (win-win). Communication skills such as listening, framing and clarity carry the strategy.

**Business letters.** Letterhead, date, reference number, inside address, salutation, subject line, body, complimentary close, signature, enclosures. The 7 Cs: clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete, courteous.

**Business reports.** Title, summary, introduction, findings, conclusions, recommendations. Structure and style affect whether a report convinces decision-makers.

## How to use this before the exam

1. **Be ready on referencing and one presentation topic.** Referencing appeared in all six 2026 papers; presentations and visual aids in five.
2. **Prepare channels of communication, non-verbal communication and negotiation as scenarios**, not just definitions.
3. **Practise two scenario answers** under a 10-minute timer using the four steps above.
4. **Keep one drafting format ready** (letter or resume) in case a Nov-Dec paper brings drafting back.

Past repetition does not guarantee what comes next. Use this list to decide what to revise first.
