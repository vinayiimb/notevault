Delhi University’s Commerce department (offering B.Com Hons and B.Com Program) is one of the most competitive in the country. The syllabus demands mastery over a diverse mix of subjects: mathematical numerical papers (like Financial Accounting, Corporate Accounting, and Income Tax), memorization-heavy law papers (like Business Law and Corporate Law), and subjective theory papers (like Management Principles).

This guide shares a tailored preparation strategy to balance these varied subjects and score a high SGPA.

---

## 1. Categorize Your B.Com Subjects

To study efficiently, divide your semester subjects into three distinct buckets:

### Bucket A: Practical/Numerical Papers
* **Subjects:** Financial Accounting, Corporate Accounting, Cost Accounting, Income Tax Law, Management Accounting, Financial Management.
* **Prep Strategy:** You cannot prepare for these by reading. You must solve practical problems daily.
* **Core Rule:** Always focus on formats (like Ledger Accounts, Balance Sheet formats, and Cash Flow Statements). Even if your final calculation goes wrong, you will receive substantial step-marking if your format and entry steps are correct.

### Bucket B: Business & Corporate Law Papers
* **Subjects:** Business Law, Corporate Law, Industrial Laws.
* **Prep Strategy:** Memorize section numbers and court case names. 
* **Core Rule:** Examiners look for legal citations. When answering a question, always structure it legally: state the relevant section (e.g., *“According to Section 2(h) of the Indian Contract Act, 1872...”*), explain the provision, describe a landmark court case if applicable (e.g., *Balfour v. Balfour*), and finally apply it to the problem statement.

### Bucket C: Management & General Theory Papers
* **Subjects:** Principles of Marketing, Human Resource Management, Business Organisation and Management.
* **Prep Strategy:** Focus on frameworks, definitions, and real-world corporate examples.
* **Core Rule:** Memorize key bullet points for theories (like Maslow’s hierarchy of needs or Porter’s five forces). Use neat block diagrams and flowcharts on the exam sheet to make your answers stand out.

## 2. Recommended Reference Books vs. PYQs

DU faculty recommend standard reference books, which are excellent for conceptual clarity:
* **Accounting:** J.R. Monga or M.C. Shukla.
* **Law:** M.C. Kuchhal or D.P. Mittal.
* **Taxation:** Dr. Vinod K. Singhania or Girish Ahuja.

However, reading these textbooks from cover to cover in the final weeks before exams is highly inefficient. Instead, use the **reverse study method**:
1. Take the last 5 years of [B.Com Previous Year Question Papers](https://www.dupyq.online/pyq-notes).
2. Identify the recurring numerical models and theoretical concepts.
3. Open your reference books *specifically* to read and practice those identified topics.

---

## 3. The Final Week Countdown

* **Days 7 to 5:** Focus on clearing doubts in numerical topics. Practice building adjustments for final accounts or tax liability calculations.
* **Days 4 to 2:** Switch entirely to Law and Theory. Write out short summaries of section provisions and practice linking them to past exam case-study questions.
* **Day 1:** Do not read new topics. Practice solving one complete 3-hour previous year paper under exam conditions to establish your pacing.
