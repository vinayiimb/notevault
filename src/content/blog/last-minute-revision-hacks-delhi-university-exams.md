It is the night before the exam, you have a 300-page textbook, and you have barely attended class. This is a situation that almost every Delhi University (DU) student faces at some point. While studying systematically throughout the semester is ideal, panic study is a skill in itself.

If you are short on time, trying to read everything will result in remembering nothing. Instead, you need a hyper-focused, tactical survival strategy. This guide shares a 3-day revision plan to help you clear your exams.

---

## 1. Apply the 80/20 Rule (Pareto Principle)

In any DU exam paper, **80% of the marks come from 20% of the syllabus**. Your goal is to find that 20% and master it.

How to find the high-yield 20%:
* Open the [DU PYQ archive](https://www.dupyq.online/pyq-notes) and pull the last three years of papers for your subject.
* Identify questions that repeat *exactly* or are minor variations of the same topic.
* Focus all your remaining energy on these recurring topics. If a topic has not appeared in the past three years, skip it completely.

---

## 2. The 3-Day Survival Schedule

If you have exactly 72 hours before the exam, structure your time like this:

### Day 1: Audit and Filtering (Hours 0 to 24)
* **Goal:** Understand what is on the exam and gather resources.
* **Morning:** Download past papers and cross-reference them with the official syllabus. List the top 5 recurring topics.
* **Afternoon:** Find readymade, short revision notes or YouTube crash course playlists. Do not touch heavy textbooks.
* **Evening:** Read through the summaries of your top 5 topics. Understand the basic concepts and key definitions.

### Day 2: High-Yield Practice (Hours 24 to 48)
* **Goal:** Learn how to write answers for the top topics.
* **Morning:** Solve the most common numerical models (for math/commerce) or draft detailed outlines for subjective questions (for humanities).
* **Afternoon:** Focus on diagrams, flowcharts, and equations. Memorize these visual cues, as they are easier to recall under stress.
* **Evening:** Review short definitions and lists of points. 

### Day 3: Pacing and Mock Simulation (Hours 48 to 72)
* **Goal:** Adapt to the exam environment and timing.
* **Morning:** Solve one full past paper in a quiet room with a timer set for 3 hours. Do not consult notes.
* **Afternoon:** Evaluate your attempt. Check if your answers were long enough and if you finished on time.
* **Evening:** Sleep. Do not pull an all-nighter right before the exam. Lack of sleep impairs memory recall and reasoning, wiping out the benefit of last-minute study.

---

## 3. Answer Sheet Presentation Tricks for Unprepared Topics

If a question appears on a topic you did not prepare for, do not leave it blank. You can still score partial marks using these steps:
1. **Analyze the Keywords:** Identify core terms in the question and write down everything you know about them in the introduction.
2. **Use Clear Visual Formats:** Present what little information you have using clean subheadings, bullet points, and simple flowcharts. A neat layout looks more organized and can earn step marks.
3. **Compare and Contrast:** If you don't know the exact concept, compare it to a related concept you do know. This shows the examiner that you understand the general academic domain.
