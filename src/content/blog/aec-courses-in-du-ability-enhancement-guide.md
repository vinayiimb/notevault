Ability Enhancement Courses (AECs) are the compulsory language and foundational papers that every Delhi University student must clear, regardless of their degree program. While most students view AECs as formalities, they carry real credit value and affect your CGPA. Ignoring them or underperforming can create an unnecessary drag on your academic record.

This guide covers what AECs are, the specific papers you will encounter, and how to clear them with top marks.

---

## What is an AEC?

An Ability Enhancement Course (AEC) is a **compulsory, 2-credit course** that focuses on language proficiency and foundational knowledge. Under the NEP-based UGCF, AECs replaced the older AECC (Ability Enhancement Compulsory Course) system.

There are two types of AECs:

### 1. Language AECs
These develop proficiency in English, Hindi, or a Modern Indian Language (MIL):
- **English Communication / Academic Writing** — Focuses on comprehension, essay writing, and communication skills.
- **Hindi (Aadhaar / Vyavaharik)** — Hindi language and basic literature.
- **MIL (Modern Indian Language)** — Regional languages like Punjabi, Urdu, Bengali, Tamil, etc., depending on the student's choice and college availability.

### 2. Environmental Studies AEC
- **Environmental Science / Environmental Studies (EVS)** — A universal paper covering ecology, pollution, biodiversity, and environmental policies. This paper is common across all streams.

---

## How AECs are Assessed

AECs follow a straightforward evaluation structure:

| Component | Weightage | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Internal Assessment | 25–30% | Attendance, assignments, class participation |
| End-Term Exam | 70–75% | Written exam (usually 2–3 hours) |

### Language AECs
- The English AEC typically includes comprehension passages, short essays, précis writing, and grammar exercises.
- Hindi / MIL papers include translation, essay writing in the chosen language, and short-answer questions on prescribed readings.

### Environmental Studies
- EVS is largely factual — definitions, classifications, and policy descriptions. Questions often repeat year after year. The key to scoring well is memorizing standard answers for topics like: biodiversity hotspots, types of pollution, the Wildlife Protection Act, and sustainable development.

## Scoring Strategy: How to Get an O Grade in AECs

### For English AEC
1. **Master the Comprehension Format:** Read the passage once for overall meaning, then re-read specifically to answer the questions. Do not copy sentences verbatim from the passage — rephrase in your own words.
2. **Practice Précis Writing:** Précis has a very specific format — it must be exactly one-third the length of the original passage, written in the third person, and in a single paragraph. Practice this with past papers.
3. **Grammar Section:** Brush up on tenses, voice (active/passive), narration (direct/indirect speech), and common idioms. This section is pure marks if you know the rules.

### For Hindi / MIL AEC
1. **Translation Practice:** If there is a Hindi-to-English or English-to-Hindi translation section, practice with newspaper articles. Focus on idiomatic accuracy rather than literal word-for-word translation.
2. **Essay Writing:** Hindi essays follow a standard format: introduction, main points, conclusion. Keep sentences short and clear.

### For EVS
1. **Download Past Papers:** EVS questions repeat extensively. Go to the [DU PYQ archive](https://www.dupyq.online/pyq-notes) and pull the last 3 years of EVS papers.
2. **Memorize Standard Lists:** Topics like "types of solid waste," "causes of water pollution," or "the three R's (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle)" appear in almost every session.
3. **Draw Diagrams:** Simple diagrams like the water cycle, food chain, or ecosystem structure earn bonus marks and fill space effectively.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Skipping AEC exams because they are "just 2 credits."** A fail or low grade in an AEC still appears on your transcript and affects your CGPA.
- **Not attending classes.** AEC attendance marks are easy points — losing them means you need a near-perfect exam score to compensate.
- **Underestimating EVS.** The content is easy, but the exam requires specific factual answers. Students who rely on general knowledge without reviewing the syllabus often score lower than expected.
