B.A. (Hons.) Political Science at Delhi University is almost entirely a long-answer, theory-writing degree — there is very little numerical or objective-type content across the programme, which makes answer structure and argument quality the deciding factor in most papers.

This guide covers how the programme's papers are structured and where to find genuine previous year question papers for every subject.

---

## What the Programme Covers

The programme runs across 8 semesters under DU's UGCF/NEP structure, combining core DSC papers in political theory, Indian government and politics, comparative politics and international relations, with DSE electives in later semesters and the university-wide AEC/SEC/VAC/GE papers.

On [DU PYQ Online](https://www.dupyq.online/papers/ba-hons-political-science), the archive for this programme currently holds **417 previous year question papers across 54 subjects**, organised by semester.

---

## Why Political Science Papers Reward a Specific Skill

### 1. Structure Beats Length
DU Political Science examiners consistently reward answers with a clear introduction, 3–4 distinctly argued points, and a conclusion — over long, unstructured answers that cover more ground but read as a single paragraph. Reading 3–4 years of a paper's previous questions makes the expected answer length and structure visible faster than any single guide can describe.

### 2. Thinkers and Theories Repeat
Political Theory and Western/Indian Political Thought papers draw from a fixed set of thinkers (Plato, Marx, Gandhi, Ambedkar, and others depending on the exact paper). Previous year papers reveal which thinkers are asked most often and in what combinations — genuinely useful information a syllabus list alone doesn't give you.

### 3. Comparative and IR Papers Need Current Examples
Comparative Politics and International Relations papers often ask you to illustrate a theoretical concept with a real-world example. Past papers show the *pattern* of these questions even though which country or event they reference changes year to year.

## How to Use Previous Year Papers for This Programme

1. Open your current semester's subjects on the [B.A. Hons Political Science papers page](https://www.dupyq.online/papers/ba-hons-political-science).
2. For each paper, read the last 3–4 years of questions before you start revising the topic itself — it tells you which sub-topics within a broad theme actually get asked.
3. Practice writing full-length answers under time pressure for at least 2 previous papers per subject, not just outlining them mentally.
4. Keep a running list of thinkers/theories that repeat across years for theory-heavy papers.

For general guidance on building a revision plan from previous year papers, see [How to Use DU Previous Year Question Papers for Semester Exams](https://www.dupyq.online/blog/how-to-use-du-previous-year-question-papers), and for answer-writing technique specifically, see [How to Write High-Scoring Answers in DU Semester Exams](https://www.dupyq.online/blog/how-to-write-high-scoring-answers-du-exams).
