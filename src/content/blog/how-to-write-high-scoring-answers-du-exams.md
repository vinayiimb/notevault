Subjective answer writing is an art, especially in Delhi University (DU) exams. Many students cover the entire syllabus but find themselves stuck in the 6.0–7.0 SGPA range. The difference between an average grade and a 9.0+ SGPA often comes down to **how** you present your answers on the answer sheet.

This guide shares the exact strategies and layouts used by top-scoring students to write answers that grab the examiner’s attention.

---

## 1. Quality vs. Quantity: The Page-Length Myth

There is a common belief in DU that filling more pages guarantees higher marks. While length *does* matter for long subjective questions (since a 15-mark question cannot be answered in half a page), writing irrelevant fluff will hurt your score.

As a general guideline:
* **5 to 6 Marks:** Write around **1.5 to 2 pages** (approx. 150–200 words).
* **8 to 10 Marks:** Write around **3 to 4 pages** (approx. 300–400 words).
* **12 to 15 Marks:** Write around **5 to 6 pages** (approx. 500–600 words).

Instead of repeating the same points, focus on expanding your answer with unique dimensions like historical context, critiques, case studies, or flowcharts.

---

## 2. The 4-Part Structure of a Perfect Answer

Examiners grade hundreds of sheets and look for structured layouts. Never write your entire answer as a single massive paragraph. Instead, divide it into four clear sections:

### I. Introduction
Start with a clear, concise definition or a brief history of the concept. For example, if the question is on *Keynesian Economics*, define the theory in 3–4 lines and mention the publication year of John Maynard Keynes's seminal work.

### II. Core Body (Points & Sub-headings)
Break the main arguments into points. Each point must have a bold sub-heading. Use bullet points rather than paragraphs.
* Underline important keywords or names of authors/thinkers.
* Use tables for comparisons (e.g., *Micro vs. Macro Economics*).

### III. Diagrams, Flowcharts, and Equations
A diagram is worth a page of text. Even in humanities subjects like Political Science or History, you can draw a mind map of causes, chronological timelines, or flowcharts of institutional structures. In Science and Commerce, ensure graphs are fully labeled and math derivations are cleanly boxed.

### IV. Conclusion
Always end with a 4–5 line balanced conclusion or future outlook. Summarize your main arguments and add a closing remark. A missing conclusion makes the answer feel incomplete.

---

## 3. Presentation Tips that Boost Marks

* **Use a Black Pen for Headings:** Write headings in black ink and the body in blue ink to make sections visually distinct.
* **Leave Lines Between Points:** Don't crowd your text. Leave a blank line before and after a subheading or diagram.
* **Cite Authors/Thinkers:** Citing thinkers or scholars (e.g., *"As argued by Harold Laski..."* or *"According to Kotler's marketing mix..."*) adds immense academic authority to your writing.

---

## Final Strategy

Before entering the exam hall, practice writing answers to previous year questions under timed conditions. Go to the [searchable PYQ archive](https://www.dupyq.online/pyq-notes) to download past papers. Take a 15-mark question and try to outline and draft a structured 5-page answer within 25 minutes. This practice will prevent your hand from cramping during the actual exam.
