B.A. (Hons.) Economics is one of the most competitive undergraduate programmes at Delhi University, and also one of the most paper-heavy — the maths-driven core papers (Statistics, Mathematical Methods, Econometrics) run alongside theory-heavy ones (Microeconomics, Macroeconomics, Indian Economy), which means two very different revision strategies inside the same degree.

This guide covers how the programme's exams are structured and where to find real previous year papers for every subject.

## What the Programme Covers

B.A. (Hons.) Economics at DU is a UGCF/NEP-structured honours programme spread across 8 semesters, combining core Discipline Specific Courses (DSC) in economic theory and quantitative methods with Discipline Specific Electives (DSE) in later semesters, plus AEC, SEC, VAC and GE papers common to all DU undergraduate programmes.

On [DU PYQ Online](https://www.dupyq.online/papers/ba-hons-economics), the catalogued archive for this programme currently holds **425 previous year question papers across 74 subjects** — covering both the quantitative core and the electives, organised by semester so you can go straight to the papers relevant to your current term.

## Why Economics Exams Are Different

### 1. Two Distinct Question Styles
Quantitative papers (Statistics, Mathematical Methods) test problem-solving under time pressure — the same numerical techniques recur across years with different data. Theory papers (Microeconomics, Macroeconomics, Indian Economy) reward structured, diagram-supported long answers. Treating both with the same revision method is the most common mistake — quant papers need timed problem practice, theory papers need answer-writing practice.

### 2. Diagrams Are Not Optional
In Microeconomics and Macroeconomics answers, a correctly labelled diagram (indifference curves, IS-LM, AD-AS) is often worth dedicated marks on its own, separate from the written explanation. Practising diagrams from real past papers, not just textbook figures, shows you which diagrams DU examiners actually ask for.

### 3. Data and Current Affairs in Indian Economy
Indian Economy papers frequently draw on recent Economic Survey and Union Budget data. Previous year papers are useful here specifically to see the *style* of question asked (compare-and-contrast, policy evaluation) even though the year-specific data itself changes.

## How to Use Previous Year Papers for This Programme

1. **Identify your semester's subjects** on the [B.A. Hons Economics papers page](https://www.dupyq.online/papers/ba-hons-economics) — each subject links to every catalogued year's paper.
2. **Separate quant and theory papers** and revise them differently — timed problem sets for the former, structured long-answer practice for the latter.
3. **Track recurring diagram questions** across 3–4 years of a theory paper; DU papers tend to repeat which diagrams are asked, even when the surrounding question changes.
4. **Cross-check the paper code** on each downloaded PDF against your own syllabus — Economics has had multiple syllabus revisions under UGCF, and papers from different years can carry different codes for what looks like the same subject.

For a general walkthrough of how to build a revision plan around previous year papers, see [How to Use DU Previous Year Question Papers for Semester Exams](https://www.dupyq.online/blog/how-to-use-du-previous-year-question-papers).
