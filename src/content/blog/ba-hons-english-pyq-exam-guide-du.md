B.A. (Hons.) English at Delhi University is organised around literary periods and genres — British Literature by century, Indian Writing in English, European Classical Literature, and more — which means each semester's papers test a genuinely different body of texts rather than building incrementally on the last.

This guide covers how the programme's papers are structured and where to find genuine previous year question papers for every subject.

---

## What the Programme Covers

The programme runs across 8 semesters under DU's UGCF/NEP structure, with core DSC papers built around specific literary periods and forms, DSE electives in later semesters, and the university-wide AEC/SEC/VAC/GE papers common to all DU undergraduate programmes.

On [DU PYQ Online](https://www.dupyq.online/papers/b-a-hons-english), the catalogued archive for this programme currently holds **404 previous year question papers across 53 subjects**, organised by semester.

---

## Why English Literature Papers Need a Different Approach

### 1. Text-Specific Recall Matters
Unlike papers built on general concepts, English literature papers expect you to quote and closely reference specific texts. Previous year papers show exactly which texts within a broad reading list get asked most often — useful when a period's reading list has more texts than you can equally prepare.

### 2. Question Phrasing Repeats More Than the Texts Do
Even when the specific text referenced changes, the *type* of question (a close reading passage, a thematic essay, a comparison between two authors) tends to repeat across years for the same paper. Recognising the pattern lets you prepare a flexible answer structure rather than memorising fixed essays.

### 3. Context and Criticism Carry Real Marks
Papers on literary theory or criticism-heavy periods often reward situating a text within its historical or critical context, not just summarising plot or theme. Past papers reveal how much weight a given paper places on this.

## How to Use Previous Year Papers for This Programme

1. Open your current semester's subjects on the [B.A. Hons English papers page](https://www.dupyq.online/papers/b-a-hons-english).
2. For each period/paper, note which texts from the reading list are asked most frequently across the last 3–4 years.
3. Practice writing timed essay-length answers, not just point-form notes — English papers are almost entirely long-form writing.
4. Keep separate short quote banks per text so you can support answers with textual evidence under exam time pressure.

For general guidance on using previous year papers effectively, see [How to Use DU Previous Year Question Papers for Semester Exams](https://www.dupyq.online/blog/how-to-use-du-previous-year-question-papers), and for humanities-specific answer-writing technique, see [BA Hons Humanities Exam Prep Guide](https://www.dupyq.online/blog/ba-hons-humanities-exam-preparation-guide-du).
