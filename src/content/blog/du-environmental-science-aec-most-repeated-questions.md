Environmental Science: Theory into Practice (I) (Unique Paper Code **2181001001**) is an Ability Enhancement Course (AEC) that students across many DU programmes sit. The syllabus is broad and the paper is short, so the real question is what to revise first.

We took every distinct paper we have for this code (17 papers, from Nov-Dec 2023 to May-Jun 2026, with duplicate copies of the same paper removed) and counted what came back. All the papers are on the [Environmental Science (I) PYQ page](https://www.dupyq.online/papers/ability-enhancement-courses/environmental-science-theory-into-practice-i).

## What the paper looks like now

In the May-Jun 2026 papers, students from the 2025 admission (and repeaters from 2023 and 2024) attempt **only Part A**: 1 hour, 30 marks.

| Question | What it asks | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 (compulsory) | (a) expand 5 abbreviations, (b) 5 true/false statements | 5 + 5 |
| Q2 to Q5 | Attempt any two of these four. Q2 is short notes (any two of three, 5 marks each). Q3 to Q5 are long answers in two parts (5 + 5) | 10 each |

Repeaters from 2022 also attempt Part B (20 marks, any two questions), and SOL repeaters add Part C. **Part B is where the data-table questions sit.**

## True or false: statements from past papers, with answers

True/false statements get recycled, and several of these were asked in more than one paper.

| Statement (sitting) | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pyramid of biomass is always upright (May-Jun 2024, May-Jun 2026) | **False** | It can be inverted, for example in a pond or the ocean. The pyramid of *energy* is always upright. |
| There are 10 SDGs (2026) · the UN formulated 15 SDGs (2023) | **False** | There are 17. |
| Only 10 percent of energy passes to the next trophic level (2026) | **True** | The 10 percent rule is the approximate figure. |
| Energy flow in an ecosystem is cyclic (Nov-Dec 2025) | **False** | Energy flow is one-way. Nutrients cycle, energy does not. |
| Food webs are simpler than food chains (2026) | **False** | A web is many interlinked chains, so it is more complex. |
| Secondary succession is faster than primary (Nov-Dec 2024) · slower (May-Jun 2024) | **True / False** | Soil is already present in secondary succession. It was asked both ways. |
| Sunderlal Bahuguna was a leader in the Bishnoi Movement (2026) | **False** | He is linked with the Chipko Movement. Bishnoi is Khejarli, Rajasthan, 1730, associated with Amrita Devi. |
| Gaura Devi took part in the Bishnoi movement (May-Jun 2025) | **False** | She is associated with Chipko (Reni village, 1974). |
| Appiko movement took place in Uttar Pradesh (2023) | **False** | It was in Karnataka (the Western Ghats). |
| Ecological pyramids were given by Arthur Tansley (May-Jun 2025) | **False** | Charles Elton introduced them. Tansley coined the word "ecosystem". |
| Pond is a lotic ecosystem (May-Jun 2025) · river is lentic (May-Jun 2024) | **False** | Ponds are lentic (still water). Rivers are lotic (flowing). |
| Ecosystem services include only provisioning, regulating and supporting (Nov-Dec 2025) | **False** | Cultural services are the fourth type. |
| Environmental studies is a purely biological discipline (Nov-Dec 2025) | **False** | It is multidisciplinary. |
| Renewable energy sources cause no environmental impact (2026) | **False** | For example, dams displace people and habitats, and solar needs land and creates panel waste. |
| Acid rain improves soil fertility (2026) | **False** | It acidifies soil and leaches nutrients. |
| Ozone depletion is caused mainly by CFCs (2026) | **True** | And the CFC phase-out is under the Montreal Protocol, not Ramsar (May-Jun 2025 asked this). |
| Sulphur dioxide is a secondary pollutant (May-Jun 2024) | **False** | It is a primary pollutant. |
| Ozone layer is found in the thermosphere (Nov-Dec 2023) | **False** | It is in the stratosphere. |
| Biodiversity increases ecosystem stability · wetlands act as natural water filters (2026) | **True** | Both are standard textbook statements. |

Fill-in-the-blank pattern to expect: *the Ramsar Convention protects wetlands*, *pyramid of energy is always upright*, *loudness is measured in decibels*.

## Answer pointers for the top topics

These are revision prompts, not full answers. Write the full answer from your textbook and class notes.

**Sustainable development.** Meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs (the Brundtland definition, 1987). Three pillars: environmental, social, economic. Link to the 17 SDGs and the 3R principle (reduce, reuse, recycle).

**Solid waste management.** Segregation at source, collection, composting of organic waste, recycling, waste-to-energy, and sanitary landfills as the last option. Add the 3Rs and the plastic and e-waste rules, then one local example.

**Renewable energy.** Solar, wind, hydro, tidal, biomass and geothermal. Advantages: low emissions and regeneration. Limitations for solar: intermittency, land use, storage cost and panel waste. Never write "no impact".

**Ecosystem services.** Four types: provisioning (food, water), regulating (climate, flood control), supporting (nutrient cycling, soil formation) and cultural (recreation, spiritual value). Give one example for each.

**Ecological succession.** Primary succession starts on bare rock with no soil: pioneer species such as lichens, then mosses, herbs, shrubs and trees, ending in a climax community. Secondary succession starts where soil already exists (abandoned farmland) and is faster. For the aquatic route, describe the hydrosere: phytoplankton, submerged plants, floating plants, reed swamp, sedge meadow, woodland.

**Ecological pyramids.** Three types: number, biomass, energy. Energy is always upright because energy is lost at each level. Number and biomass can be inverted (a tree supporting many insects; phytoplankton supporting zooplankton in a pond).

**Air pollution.** Primary pollutants (SO2, NOx, CO, particulate matter) vs secondary pollutants (ground-level ozone, PAN, sulphuric acid). Effects on health, crops and buildings. Controls: ESPs and scrubbers, catalytic converters, cleaner fuels, public transport, and the National Clean Air Programme.

**Environmental movements.** Chipko (1973, Uttarakhand, led by Chandi Prasad Bhatt and Sunderlal Bahuguna, with women like Gaura Devi), Bishnoi (1730, Khejarli, Rajasthan), Appiko (1983, Karnataka), Silent Valley (Kerala) and Narmada Bachao Andolan. For each, give year, place, cause and outcome.

**Data-table questions.** Read the table first. The usual sub-parts: name the highest or lowest, calculate a percentage or total, explain two reasons, then suggest two or three measures. Practise the layout, because the marks are mostly for reading the data correctly and linking it to a concept.

## How to use this in the last week

1. **Learn the abbreviation table and the true/false table.** Together they are 10 of the 30 marks, and the questions are the most repeated part of the paper.
2. **Write one clean answer each** for sustainable development, solid waste, renewable energy and ecosystem services. Those four appeared in 9 to 13 of the 17 papers each.
3. **Prepare short notes on** succession, pyramids, and one environmental movement. The short-note question is usually drawn from these.
4. **Do one data-table question** from Nov-Dec 2025 or May-Jun 2026 under time.

Past repetition does not guarantee what comes next. Use this list to decide what to revise first, not what to skip.
