With the implementation of the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020, Delhi University introduced the **Undergraduate Curriculum Framework (UGCF)**. This system replaced the older Choice Based Credit System (CBCS) and changed how degrees are structured, how credits are earned, and how papers are selected.

For many students, navigating the mix of Core Courses, Electives, and Skill papers can feel overwhelming. This guide breaks down the UGCF structure so you can choose your papers and understand your credit requirements.

---

## 1. The Core Classifications of UGCF Papers

Under the UGCF, your semester syllabus is divided into several course types, each designed to provide a balance of specialized depth and multidisciplinary breadth:

### DSC: Discipline Specific Core
* **What it is:** These are your main core subjects. If you are doing B.Com (Hons), subjects like Financial Accounting or Corporate Law are DSCs.
* **Significance:** DSCs are compulsory and represent the core academic domain of your degree.

### DSE: Discipline Specific Elective
* **What it is:** These are elective papers offered within your main discipline.
* **Significance:** You select from a list of options, allowing you to specialize in areas like Finance, Human Resources, or Marketing within a general Commerce degree.

### GE: Generic Elective
* **What it is:** These are elective papers offered by *other* departments.
* **Significance:** If you are a Commerce student, you can take a GE in Economics, History, Computer Science, or English. It encourages cross-disciplinary learning.

---

## 2. Skill and Value-Addition Modules

To build practical skills and general knowledge, the UGCF includes three additional course categories:

### SEC: Skill Enhancement Course
* **What it is:** Practical, skill-oriented papers (e.g., Digital Marketing, Financial Database Analysis, Creative Writing).
* **Significance:** These papers are highly hands-on and often include practical or project-based marking.

### VAC: Value Addition Course
* **What it is:** Courses centered on ethics, values, culture, and constitution (e.g., Constitutional Values, Vedic Mathematics, Fit India).
* **Significance:** These are light, 2-credit courses aimed at holistic student development.

### AEC: Ability Enhancement Course
* **What it is:** Compulsory language or environmental studies papers (e.g., Environmental Science, Hindi, or alternative regional languages).

---

## 3. How the Credit System Works

Each paper in the UGCF carries a specific credit value, representing the number of lecture, tutorial, or practical hours per week:
* **DSC/DSE/GE papers:** Typically carry **4 credits** each (representing 3 lectures and 1 tutorial per week).
* **SEC/VAC papers:** Typically carry **2 credits** each.
* **AEC papers:** Typically carry **2 credits** each.

To earn your degree, you must compile a minimum credit score. A typical 3-year undergraduate degree requires completing **132 credits**, while a 4-year degree (with Research/Honours) requires **176 credits**.

---

## 4. The Multiple Entry and Exit System (MEES)

One of the key features of the NEP under UGCF is the flexibility of entry and exit points:
* **Exit after 1 Year:** Receive a **Certificate** in your discipline (minimum 44 credits).
* **Exit after 2 Years:** Receive a **Diploma** in your discipline (minimum 88 credits).
* **Exit after 3 Years:** Receive a standard **Bachelor's Degree** (minimum 132 credits).
* **Complete 4 Years:** Receive a **Bachelor's Degree with Honours/Research** (minimum 176 credits).
