## Q1
meta: MCQ | 1 | B
tags: Microeconomics | Perfect competition & partial equilibrium
When the supply curve $S_x$ is backward bending and the demand curve $D_x$ is downward sloping as shown in the figure (in the paper), there are two equilibria $M$ and $N$, respectively ($M$ at the higher price $P_{x_M}$ and smaller quantity $x_M$; $N$ at the lower price $P_{x_N}$ and larger quantity $x_N$). Which of the following statements is CORRECT?
(A) Only $M$ is stable equilibrium
(B) Only $N$ is stable equilibrium
(C) Both $M$ and $N$ are stable equilibria
(D) Both $M$ and $N$ are unstable equilibria
---
**Basics first.** An equilibrium is **stable** if, after a small disturbance, the market moves back to it. Under the usual **Walrasian (price) adjustment**, price rises when there is excess demand and falls when there is excess supply. So an equilibrium is stable when excess demand \(ED(p) = Q_d(p) - Q_s(p)\) **falls** as price rises:
\[
\frac{dED}{dp} = \frac{1}{\text{slope of } D} - \frac{1}{\text{slope of } S} < 0,
\]
where the slopes are measured as \(dP/dx\) in the usual diagram.

**Solution.** In the figure, the backward-bending supply curve lies above the straight demand line between \(M\) and \(N\).
- At **N**, the supply curve is downward sloping and **steeper** than demand. With slope of D \(= -a\) and slope of S \(= -b\), \(b > a\): \(\dfrac{dED}{dp} = -\dfrac1a + \dfrac1b < 0\). A price above \(P_N\) creates excess supply and the price falls back: **stable**.
- At **M**, the supply curve is **flatter** than demand (\(b < a\)), so \(\dfrac{dED}{dp} > 0\): a price above \(P_M\) creates excess demand and pushes the price further up: **unstable**.

(Under Marshallian quantity adjustment the conclusion reverses; the standard price-adjustment test gives only \(N\) stable, which is the official answer.)

**Answer: (B)**

**Exam takeaway:** For a backward-bending supply curve, Walrasian stability requires supply to be steeper than demand at the equilibrium.

## Q2
meta: MCQ | 1 | D
tags: Indian Economy | External sector & public finance
Which of the following deficits indicates the true current fiscal position of the Indian Economy?
(A) Revenue Deficit
(B) Capital Deficit
(C) Current Account Deficit
(D) Primary Deficit
---
**Basics first.**
- **Fiscal deficit** = total expenditure − total non-debt receipts (the government's total borrowing need).
- **Revenue deficit** = revenue expenditure − revenue receipts.
- **Primary deficit** = fiscal deficit − interest payments.
- **Current account deficit** is an external-sector (balance of payments) concept, not a fiscal one.

**Solution.** Interest payments are the cost of **past** borrowing. Removing them from the fiscal deficit shows how much the government needs to borrow because of **this year's** decisions alone. That is why the primary deficit is said to show the true current fiscal position.

**Answer: (D)**

**Exam takeaway:** Primary deficit = fiscal deficit minus interest; a zero primary deficit means current borrowing only pays interest on old debt.

## Q3
meta: MCQ | 1 | A
tags: Statistics | Mathematical statistics
Which of the following CORRECTLY defines the relationship between the variances of sample means for simple random samples drawn with and without replacement from a normal population?
(A) $\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n} > \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\left(\dfrac{N-n}{N-1}\right)$
(B) $\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n} \le \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\left(\dfrac{N-n}{N-1}\right)$
(C) $\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n} < \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\left(\dfrac{N-n}{N-1}\right)$
(D) $\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n} = \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\left(\dfrac{N-n}{N-1}\right)$
---
**Basics first.**
- **With replacement** (or an infinite population): \(Var(\bar x) = \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\).
- **Without replacement** from a finite population of size \(N\): \(Var(\bar x) = \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\cdot\dfrac{N-n}{N-1}\). The factor \(\dfrac{N-n}{N-1}\) is the **finite population correction**.

**Solution.** For \(n > 1\), \(N - n < N - 1\), so \(\dfrac{N-n}{N-1} < 1\). Sampling without replacement gives a **smaller** variance, because each draw removes a unit and later draws cannot repeat it:
\[
\frac{\sigma^2}{n} > \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\left(\frac{N-n}{N-1}\right).
\]

**Answer: (A)**

**Exam takeaway:** The finite population correction is less than 1, so sampling without replacement is more precise.

## Q4
meta: MCQ | 1 | B
tags: Macroeconomics | National income accounting
Suppose that one million unemployed persons in a country are receiving Rs. 6000 per month per person as an unemployment allowance. If the government, instead of paying unemployment allowance, hires all of them at the same amount (Rs. 6000 per month per person) and engages them in digging the pits and filling the same pits. What will be the effect on GDP?
(A) No effect on GDP
(B) GDP will rise.
(C) GDP will fall.
(D) The effect on GDP will be uncertain.
---
**Basics first.**
- An **unemployment allowance** is a **transfer payment**: money given without any current good or service in return. It is not part of GDP.
- **Wages paid by the government for work** buy a government service. GDP values government services **at cost**, i.e. at the wages paid, regardless of how useful the work is.

**Solution.** The same money is now paid as wages for (government) services instead of as a transfer. GDP rises by \(10^6 \times 6000 =\) Rs 600 crore per month, even though the pits are filled back in. (This is Keynes's famous point that even "useless" government spending raises measured output and incomes.)

**Answer: (B)**

**Exam takeaway:** Transfers are excluded from GDP; government purchases of labour services are included at cost.

## Q6
meta: MCQ | 1 | all
tags: Mathematics | Linear algebra
Let $W$ be a subspace of a vector space $\mathbb R^3$. Then, which of the following sets of vectors forms a basis of $W$?
(A) $(1, 2, 1)$ and $(1, -2, 5)$
(B) $(1, 3, 2), (1, -1, 0), (4, -1, 0)$ and $(3, 1, -3)$
(C) $(1, 1, 1), (1, 2, 3)$ and $(2, -1, 1)$
(D) $(1, -2, 1), (2, 1, -1)$ and $(7, -4, 1)$
---
**Basics first.** A **basis** of a vector space is a set of vectors that is **linearly independent** and **spans** the space. In \(\mathbb R^3\) a basis has exactly 3 vectors; a 2-dimensional subspace (a plane) has a basis of 2 vectors.

**Solution.**
- **(A)** \((1,2,1)\) and \((1,-2,5)\) are not multiples of each other, so they are independent: a basis of the plane they span.
- **(B)** Four vectors in \(\mathbb R^3\) are always linearly dependent: never a basis.
- **(C)** \(\det\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&2&3\\2&-1&1\end{pmatrix} = 1(5) - 1(-5) + 1(-5) = 5 \ne 0\): independent, a basis of \(\mathbb R^3\).
- **(D)** \((7,-4,1) = 3(1,-2,1) + 2(2,1,-1)\): dependent, not a basis.

Because the question never says what \(W\) is, both (A) (for a plane) and (C) (for \(W = \mathbb R^3\)) can be bases. The question is ill-posed, and IIT awarded **marks to all**.

**Answer: Marks to all**

**Exam takeaway:** Test independence with the determinant; a basis must also match the dimension of the space.

## Q8
meta: MCQ | 1 | C
tags: Statistics | Hypothesis testing
In the hypothesis testing, which of the following defines the size of power of the test?
(A) 1 – (Probability of accepting null hypothesis when it is true)
(B) 1 – (Probability of rejecting null hypothesis when it is true)
(C) 1 – (Probability of accepting null hypothesis when it is false)
(D) 1 + (Probability of rejecting null hypothesis when it is not true)
---
**Basics first.**
- **Type I error:** rejecting \(H_0\) when it is true; probability \(\alpha\) (the size).
- **Type II error:** accepting \(H_0\) when it is false; probability \(\beta\).
- **Power** \(= P(\text{reject } H_0 \mid H_0 \text{ false}) = 1 - \beta\).

**Solution.** Power \(= 1 - P(\text{Type II error}) = 1 - P(\text{accepting } H_0 \text{ when it is false})\). Options (A) and (B) are built from probabilities computed when \(H_0\) is **true** (they relate to the size \(\alpha\)), and (D) can exceed 1, which no probability can.

**Answer: (C)**

**Exam takeaway:** Power = 1 − β, the probability of correctly rejecting a false null.

Let $x^3 + 3y^2 = 4$ for all $x, y \in \mathbb R$, $y' = \dfrac{dy}{dx}$ and $y'' = \dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$. Then
(A) $x^2 + yy'' + (y')^2 = 0$
(B) $2x + y'' + 2(y')^2 = 0$

## Q13
meta: MCQ | 2 | B
tags: Mathematics | Preliminaries and functions
Let $f : [0, \infty) \to \mathbb R$ be a function defined by $f(x) = \dfrac{x+1}{x+2}$ for all $x$. Then $f$ is
(A) one-one and onto.
(B) one-one but not onto.
(C) onto but not one-one.
(D) neither one-one nor onto.
---
**Basics first.**
- **One-one (injective):** different inputs give different outputs. A strictly increasing function is one-one.
- **Onto (surjective):** every element of the codomain (here \(\mathbb R\)) is reached by some input.

**Solution.** \(f'(x) = \dfrac{(x+2) - (x+1)}{(x+2)^2} = \dfrac{1}{(x+2)^2} > 0\), so \(f\) is strictly increasing and therefore one-one.
Its values start at \(f(0) = \tfrac12\) and rise towards 1 but never reach it, so the range is \([\tfrac12, 1)\). Since the codomain is all of \(\mathbb R\), \(f\) is not onto.

**Answer: (B)**

**Exam takeaway:** Compare the range with the stated codomain to decide "onto".

Let the function $f : \mathbb R^2 \to \mathbb R$ be $f(x, y) = \dfrac{xy^2}{x^3 + 2x^2y + y^3}$, $f(0, 0) = 0$. Then
